# 7-Day Rotating Meal Plan — 13-Month-Old (Vegetarian)

Built around the foods you're already making, with additions to close the iron, B12, omega-3, and zinc gaps common in vegetarian toddlers.

---

## Milk Schedule (18–22 oz total)

| Time | Amount |
|---|---|
| Morning (with or after breakfast) | ~6 oz |
| Afternoon snack | ~6 oz |
| Bedtime | ~6–8 oz |

**Key rule:** keep milk away from iron-heavy meals (dal, ragi, rajma, chana, spinach). Calcium blocks iron absorption. Water with meals instead.

---

## Day 1

- **Breakfast** — Banana oatmeal + ½ tsp ground flaxseed
- **Snack** — Raw homemade paneer cubes + orange segments
- **Lunch** — Khichdi (moong dal, carrot, beans) with ghee + squeeze of lemon
- **Snack** — Yogurt with mashed mango or berries
- **Dinner** — Sweet potato & paneer pancakes + soft-cooked vegetables

## Day 2

- **Breakfast** — Ragi porridge with jaggery + ghee
- **Snack** — Banana + a few soaked, chopped raisins
- **Lunch** — Curd rice with grated carrot, ghee-jeera tempering
- **Snack** — Besan chilla strips
- **Dinner** — Dal + roti torn small, mashed with ghee

## Day 3

- **Breakfast** — Besan chilla with grated zucchini/carrot + yogurt
- **Snack** — Papaya or chikoo
- **Lunch** — Rajma mashed well + rice + chopped tomato
- **Snack** — Raw paneer + soft cucumber sticks
- **Dinner** — Vegetable and moong dal soup with soft rice or dalia

## Day 4

- **Breakfast** — Poha with peas, carrot, lemon
- **Snack** — Yogurt + mashed banana
- **Lunch** — Paneer bhurji + soft paratha torn into pieces
- **Snack** — Steamed sweet potato fingers with ghee
- **Dinner** — Ragi or millet khichdi

## Day 5

- **Breakfast** — Idli with ghee, tiny bit of mild dal for dipping
- **Snack** — Mashed avocado on toast strips
- **Lunch** — Soft vegetable pulao + yogurt
- **Snack** — Chana sattu halwa (date-sweetened)
- **Dinner** — Dal dalia (broken wheat khichdi)

## Day 6

- **Breakfast** — Suji upma with vegetables
- **Snack** — Raw paneer + strawberries or orange
- **Lunch** — Kala chana mashed + rice + ghee
- **Snack** — Dhokla pieces or yogurt
- **Dinner** — Stuffed paratha (methi / palak / paneer) + curd

## Day 7

- **Breakfast** — Moong dal cheela or plain dosa with ghee
- **Snack** — Fruit bowl (banana, papaya, berries)
- **Lunch** — Mild palak paneer (no chili) + rice
- **Snack** — Chana sattu halwa (date-sweetened) or oats-banana pancake
- **Dinner** — Khichdi + yogurt (light reset day)

## Protein Reality Check

Daily need at this age: roughly **11–13g**.

| Source | Protein |
|---|---|
| 20 oz whole milk | ~12g |
| ½ cup yogurt | ~4g |
| 30g paneer | ~5g |
| ½ cup dal | ~4g |

He is already at roughly double the requirement. **Protein is not the gap — do not add more at the expense of iron-rich and fat-rich foods.**

---

## Brain Development Priorities

**1. DHA — the biggest vegetarian gap**
The brain is ~60% fat and DHA is its key structural fat. Flax, chia, and walnut provide ALA, but conversion to DHA is only ~1–5%. Without fish, an **algae-derived DHA supplement** is the only direct source. Discuss with your pediatrician.

**2. Choline — memory and neural development**
Under-consumed by most people. Vegetarian sources to work in weekly:
- Tofu and soy
- Rajma, chana, lobia
- Milk and yogurt
- Quinoa, wheat germ
- Broccoli, cauliflower
- Peanut butter (thinned)
- Egg yolk, if eggs are part of your diet — one yolk covers most of a toddler's daily need

**3. Iron** — deficiency in the first two years has documented lasting cognitive effects. See iron anchors above.

**4. Iodine** — drives thyroid function and brain development. Use iodized salt; dairy contributes.

**5. B12** — nerve development. The classic vegetarian gap; supplement worth discussing.

**6. Total fat** — not a nutrient to restrict at this age. Ghee, avocado, nut butters, full-fat dairy, coconut.

---

## Physical Growth Priorities

- **Calories and fat** over protein — toddlers grow on energy density
- **Vitamin D** (400 IU) + calcium for bone
- **Zinc** — pumpkin seed powder, chana, sesame, whole grains
- **Whole milk, not reduced-fat**, through age 2

## Weekly Iron Anchors

These are the meals doing the heavy lifting. Pair each with vitamin C (lemon, tomato, amla, orange, bell pepper):

- **Chana sattu halwa (date-sweetened) — Days 5, 7.** Your best single item: roasted chana flour plus dates stacks iron on iron, with protein and fiber and no refined sugar.
- Ragi porridge — Days 2, 4
- Rajma — Day 3
- Kala chana — Day 6
- Palak — Days 6, 7
- Dal in some form — most days
- Raisins — Day 2

## Daily Add-Ins

- **Ghee** — in nearly everything; toddlers need the fat
- **Ground flaxseed / chia / walnut powder** — ½ tsp stirred into oatmeal, porridge, or yogurt for omega-3
- **Pumpkin seed powder** — sprinkle for zinc

## Rotation Notes

- Run the same 7 days for 2–3 weeks, then swap 2–3 items to keep variety without overwhelming him
- Easy swaps: uttapam ↔ dosa, dalia ↔ khichdi, poha ↔ upma, rajma ↔ chana ↔ lobia
- **Work tofu in once or twice a week** — swap it for paneer in the bhurji, or cube it soft into khichdi. It carries choline, iron, and calcium at once.
- **Add soft-steamed broccoli or cauliflower florets** to any khichdi, soup, or pulao for choline
- If he rejects something, offer it again in a week — takes 8–15 exposures for a toddler to accept a new food

## Safety

- No whole nuts, whole grapes, popcorn, raw carrot chunks
- Very light salt, minimal added sugar
- Honey is fine now (over 12 months)

## Ask Your Pediatrician

- Vitamin D supplement (400 IU/day is standard)
- B12 supplement — relevant for vegetarian toddlers even with dairy
- Hemoglobin check to confirm iron levels are on track
